Where each group looked

Each panel is one group's attention spread over a 3×3 grid, averaged per person so nobody who produced more samples counts for more. All three are smoothed with the same blur, sized to the least precise source, and share one colour scale — so a darker cell means more attention, in every panel.

Where the two human groups differ

Measured gaze minus tapped guesses, cell by cell. Red means people actually looked there more than they said they would; blue means they said it more than they looked.

Attention per cell, with uncertainty

The same numbers as a dot plot. The line through each dot is a 95% interval across participants: where the intervals for two groups do not overlap, the difference is unlikely to be noise. Wide bars mean few people, not a strong finding.

How closely the maps agree

Correlation between each pair of maps, judged against a ceiling: how well a group agrees with itself when split in half. No pair can be expected to beat that, so the percentage of the ceiling is the honest figure.
