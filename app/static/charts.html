What this says

Where each group looked

Each panel is one group's attention spread over a 3×3 grid, averaged per person so nobody who produced more samples counts for more. All three are smoothed with the same blur, sized to the least precise source, and share one colour scale — so a darker cell means more attention, in every panel.

How closely they agree

Every pair at once. The diagonal is each source against itself, split in half — no pair can be expected to beat that, so it is the number the rest are read against.

Where they differ

A correlation says how much two sources disagree. These say where. Each cell is the second source's share of attention minus the first's: red means the second looked there more, blue means the first did.

When they looked there

The same maps, cut into three equal parts of each person's own looking — the first third of a tap sequence against the first third of a viewing window against the model's first fixations. By position, not by clock: the model has no clock and the people have no fixation count. The number under each part is how closely the pair agreed at that point.

One round against another

Two sittings of the same picture, per source — the tap group from one against the tap group from the other, rather than a pooled average that would hide which of them moved.
